I am a senior software engineer in Munich, where I have lived since January 2026. My
last contract, the shared frontend platform at Alaska Airlines, ended the same month we
moved. Since then I have been building: a Rust and PostgreSQL ingestion backend that keeps
imported values non-canonical until a curator accepts them, the concurrent scraper that
feeds it, a robot telemetry console that treats silence as an event, a reproducible
text-to-speech pipeline, and this site. Each has a page under [projects](/projects) stating
what was checked against source and what was not. I am looking for a permanent role and I
am available now.

My first engineering work was greenhouse monitoring. I wrote Python agents that
read pH and conductivity probes over FTDI serial, pushed a reference temperature
to each probe before every reading, and fanned the results out to independent sinks so that losing one
service did not lose the data. Full-stack product work came next, at Migo, then
Lululemon, then Volume Media, where I led the incremental migration of a
four-thousand-file Django monolith onto Next.js one page at a time rather than
in one cutover. After that came the deepest backend work I have done, on Nike's
consent platform: event-driven and serverless across four AWS regions including
China. I also got a taste for huge ETL jobs when I migrated roughly twenty million
consent records off a third-party vendor and fixed the failure modes the first attempt
had surfaced. Most recently, shared frontend platforms at Alaska Airlines, where
I architected Borealis, a Lit and Web Components platform that four to five product teams
adopted across Next.js, Svelte, React, Vue, and .NET.

I prefer boring tooling and explicit contracts. What I am most useful for is the
unglamorous half of platform work: migration planning, integration boundaries, versioned
contracts, test infrastructure, and documentation good enough that another team
can ship against it without me in the room. Borealis held together across five
frameworks because its props and events carried semver and a deprecation policy.
Consuming teams could upgrade on their own schedule instead of on
mine. I have kept daily engineering notes for about a decade, so the write-up
usually exists before anyone asks for it. I have taught alongside the engineering
for most of my career. I worked for six years as a substitute instructor at the
University of Washington and a teaching fellowship before it.

I hold an EU Blue Card, so I need no sponsorship in Germany. I am a native English
speaker and I am learning German. My last three roles were contracts, which let me work
across three very different platforms in quick succession. In my next role I want one
platform owned over years rather than months. I also photograph and am learning to play
the cello. Code is on [GitHub](https://github.com/rsstdd), and
[email](mailto:rssmtdd@gmail.com) is the fastest way to reach me.
