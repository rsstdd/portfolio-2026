My first engineering work was greenhouse monitoring. I wrote Python agents that
read pH and conductivity probes over FTDI serial, pushed a reference temperature
to each probe before every reading because uncompensated pH drifts with water
temperature, and fanned the results out to independent sinks so that losing one
service did not lose the data. Everything since has been a version of that same
problem at a larger scale. Full-stack product work came next, at Migo, then
Lululemon, then Volume Media, where I led the incremental migration of a
four-thousand-file Django monolith onto Next.js one page at a time rather than
in one cutover. After that came the deepest backend work I have done, on Nike's
consent platform: event-driven and serverless across four AWS regions including
China, where I migrated roughly twenty million consent records off a third-party
vendor and fixed the failure modes the first attempt had surfaced. Most
recently, shared frontend platforms at Alaska Airlines, where I architected
Borealis, a Lit and Web Components platform that four to five product teams
adopted across Next.js, Svelte, React, Vue, and .NET. The range is wider than
most and none of it was planned.

I prefer boring tooling and explicit contracts, and I would rather check
something than reason about it. What I am most useful for is the unglamorous
half of platform work: migration planning, integration boundaries, versioned
contracts, test infrastructure, and documentation good enough that another team
can ship against it without me in the room. Borealis held together across five
frameworks because its props and events carried semver and a deprecation policy,
which meant consuming teams could upgrade on their own schedule instead of on
mine. The same instinct shows up in how I handle being wrong: when a major
integration went badly, I ran the retro that turned it into readiness gates and
component contract standards the organization adopted. I have kept daily
engineering notes for about a decade, so the write-up usually exists before
anyone asks for it. I have taught alongside the engineering for most of that
time, six years as a substitute instructor at the University of Washington and
a teaching fellowship before it, and that is where most of the documentation
habit came from.

I live in Munich on an EU Blue Card. We moved here in January, when my wife
accepted a role in the city. English is native, German is A0, and there is no
point dressing that up. My last three engagements were contracts, which was
deliberate at the time because it let me work across three very different
platforms in quick succession; what I want next is the opposite of that pattern,
which is one platform owned over years rather than months. At the moment I am
building an aircraft catalog that begins at a Rust scraper into PostgreSQL
behind a typed Rust REST API and a React frontend, because I wanted a pipeline
that was real rather than illustrative. It is not finished yet, and the project
page says so. I also photograph and am learning to play the cello. Code is on
[GitHub](https://github.com/rsstdd), and [email](mailto:rssmtdd@gmail.com) is
the fastest way to reach me.
